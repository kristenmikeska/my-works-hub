He found her in the hall four minutes later, by the photographs, where she'd gone to stand somewhere that wasn't the front room for a second, because her whole body was still ringing from bar nine and she didn't trust her face.

He didn't say anything. He took her wrist, two fingers, the way he'd taken it on the landing in Syracuse a hundred years ago in November, and walked her down the hall past the kitchen and the noise and Hyein's voice saying *where are they going, Hanni, where are they GOING,* and into her room, and shut the door with his heel.

The party went to a hum through the wall. The four hundred windows across the street were all lit for the last night of the year, every one of them, like the building had been waiting up for her.

> **solar:** "What are we doing."

> **haechan:** "Sit."

> **solar:** "Haechan, it's twenty to—"

> **haechan:** "I know what time it is, princess. I'm the only person in this apartment who knows what time it is. Sit."

She sat on the window seat. He took his headphones from round his neck, the heavy black ones, warm, and held them out to her, and when she didn't take them right away he put them on her himself, carefully, moving her hair out of the way with one finger like he was clearing dust off a record.

> **solar:** "Is this the bridge? You said the bridge was—"

> **haechan:** "It's not the bridge. Just listen."

He pressed something on his phone and sat down on the floor with his back against the bed frame, facing her, his forearms on his knees, and looked at the carpet. Not at her. That was how she knew it was something.

It started with nothing. Then under the nothing a hum, very low, so low she felt it in her teeth before she heard it, and she knew it before her mind did, the way you know a smell.

*[if she went on the 2 a.m. walk]* Fifth Avenue at two in the morning. The boilers. The low B, ten thousand of them at once, the whole avenue breathing in one note. Then the breath under her boots that was a train somewhere in the dark under Lexington. Then the tick, small, electric, the light at Seventy-Second turning green for nobody. He'd had the recorder on. Of course he had. He'd stood on the white sidewalk and told her to *listen*, and the whole time he'd been listening too, for this.
*[if she didn't]* The radiator in her room at Alpha, knocking twice, the way it had every morning since August. The bell on the quad. Snow, which she hadn't known had a sound, until she heard him find it.

*[if term-ask, and he asked]* Then, small, off to one side, a laugh. Hers. On Fifth Avenue, at the corner, in the cold, when she'd said *if that's your way of sending an invitation, you failed* and he'd laughed once at the snow. He'd asked. She remembered now, him holding the little recorder up an inch, *can I keep that,* and her saying *keep what,* and him saying *never mind.* He'd kept it.
*[if term-none]* There was no voice in it anywhere. Not hers. She noticed the shape of where it wasn't, the way she'd noticed his own all year, and understood that he'd left it out on purpose, because she'd said *nothing, no recordings, not for now,* and he'd built the whole thing around the hole.

And then the piano. Four bars of it, the thing from the twenty-sixth, the one he'd said was *nothing, it's a thing I do,* sitting on top of the city like it had been written to go there, which, she realized, it had.

It went on for a while. She didn't know how long. She sat with her hands in her lap and four hundred windows behind her and listened to the city she'd grown up in sound like somewhere she'd want to live.

Then it all dropped out, the boilers and the train and the piano, all at once, the way he cut things in the studio, clean, and in the silence there was a voice. His. Close to the mic, low, flat, a little rough, the way he sounded at three in the morning when he'd forgotten anyone was listening.

> *"So. This is the part where you look at me. Don't. I'm on the floor. I'm looking at your carpet."*

She looked at him. He was on the floor, looking at her carpet.

> *"I don't say things. You know that. You've known that since the car. So I made it instead, because that's the only way I've ever been any good at saying anything, and you deserve the good version."*

> *"It's four minutes long. I timed it so you'd still have six minutes left to say no and get back out there before the ball."*

> *"Be my girlfriend, Solar."*

> *"That's it. That's the whole track."*

The silence after it was the real kind. Not the cut. The room's own. The radiator under the window, the party through the wall, Hyein laughing at something, the four hundred windows.

She took the headphones off. She held them in both hands. They were still warm.

He looked up.

**A. (Put the headphones down. Kiss him.)** *flag: epi-r-ask-kiss*
She put them down on the window seat very carefully, like they might go off, and got down off it onto the floor, onto her knees in her dress in front of him, and took his face in both her hands and kissed him. She didn't say anything. She'd decided on the window seat that she wasn't going to say anything, because he'd said it the only way he knew how and she was going to answer the only way she knew he'd hear. He made a sound against her mouth, small, surprised, like something had been let out of him, and his hands came up and held on to her wrists, not to stop her, just to hold on.
> **haechan:** "...That's a yes."
> **solar:** "That's a yes."
> **haechan:** "Okay. Just checking. I'm bad at reading you."
> **solar:** "You're the best at reading me. You're the only one who can."
> **haechan:** "Yeah. I know. I just wanted to hear you say it."

She laughed. She couldn't help it. She laughed with the headphones in her lap and her eyes going hot, and he watched her do it from the floor with the very corner of his mouth doing something he couldn't stop.
> **solar:** "Say it."
> **haechan:** "I said it. It's on the track. Track's the official version."
> **solar:** "Haechan. Say it to my face."
He looked at her for a long second. Then he said it, flat, the way he said things he'd already decided were true, and it didn't sound flat at all.
> **haechan:** "Be my girlfriend, princess."

**C. "Come here. Say it again. Not on a recording."** *flag: epi-r-ask-said*
He got up off the floor. It took him a second; his knees had gone stiff, or he was buying time, she couldn't tell. He sat down next to her on the window seat with the four hundred windows behind them both, and didn't look at the carpet anymore. He looked at her.
> **haechan:** "Be my girlfriend."
It came out different from the track. Not flat. His voice went somewhere at the end of it she'd never heard it go, a quarter-tone off, like the note on the second *stay* in the studio, and he heard it too, and he didn't take it back.
> **solar:** "Yes."
> **haechan:** "Yeah?"
> **solar:** "Yes. Obviously yes. You're an idiot, yes."

> **haechan:** "Like what."
> **solar:** "Like I just handed you something."
> **haechan:** "You did, though."

*(all three, after)*
Somebody banged on the door. Jaemin, from the sound of it, the flat of his hand, *TEN MINUTES, LOVEBIRDS, THE BALL WAITS FOR NO MAN,* and Hyein behind him saying *don't call them LOVEBIRDS, Jaemin, they'll never come out,* and Hanni saying *leave them alone,* and all of it fading back down the hall toward the television.

He stood up and held his hand down to her.

> **haechan:** "Come on. I've got a thing at midnight."

> **solar:** "Another thing? You just did a thing."

> **haechan:** "That was the eleven-forty thing. The midnight thing's different. The midnight thing's seven seconds early."

She didn't ask. She took his hand and let him pull her up, and kept it, all the way down the hall, past the photographs where she was always at the edge, into the front room, where everyone she loved was waiting for the year to end.
